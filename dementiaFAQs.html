Dementia FAQ's

Terminology

What is dementia?
Dementia is a thinking problem that limits day-to-day activities (e.g., shopping, housekeeping, accounting, food preparation, and transportation). The spectrum of thinking problems includes subjective cognitive decline, minor neurocognitive disorder (mild cognitive impairment), and major neurocognitive disorder (dementia).

Is Alzheimer disease the same thing as dementia?
No, Alzheimer disease is NOT the same thing as dementia: dementia refers to symptoms, Alzheimer disease refers to cause. Patients with Alzheimer disease invariably develop dementia, however, not all patients with dementia have Alzheimer disease, there are many other causes.

What is Alzheimer disease?
Alzheimer disease refers to amyloid plaques, also tau tangles. Amyloid plaques and tau tangles are protein aggregates only visible with a microscope.

Amyloid is unique to Alzheimer disease, however, tau has a better correlate with symptoms:
- memory centers --> typical Alzheimer disease
- vision centers --> visual-variant Alzheimer disease (i.e., posterior cortical atrophy)
- language centers --> language-variant Alzheimer disease (i.e., logopenic variant primary progressive aphasia)
- decision-making centers --> behavioral-variant Alzheimer disease

What are other causes of dementia?
Neurodegenerative causes of dementia include:
- Lewy-body disease (LBD)
- Parkinson disease dementia (PDD)
- frontotemporal dementia (FTD)
- corticobasal degeneration (CBD)
- progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP)
- primary age-related tauopathy (PART)
- limbic-predominant age-related TDP-43 encephalopathy (LATE)
- argyrophilic grain disease (AGD)
- prion disease

Nutritional causes of dementia include:
- vitamin B12 deficiency
- folate deficiency
- niacin deficiency

Toxic causes of dementia include:
- alcohol-related dementia
- heavy metal poisoning

Metabolic causes of dementia include:
- severe liver disease
- severe chronic kidney disease

Genetic causes of dementia include:
- single-gene variants of typical dementias
- cerebral autosomal dominant arteriopathy with subcortical infarcts (CADASIL)
- adrenoleukodystrophy (ALD)

Neurosurgical causes of dementia include:
- normal-pressure hydrocephalus (NPH)
- frontobasal meningioma
- chronic subdural hematoma

Rapidly-progressive causes of dementia include:
- Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease
- atypical presentation of typical dementia
- central nervous system infection
- central nervous system vasculitis
- autoimmune encephalitis
- Hashimoto encephalopathy
- central nervous system lymphoma
- paraneoplastic disease

Acute causes of encephalopathy include:
- thalamic stroke
- Wernicke encephalopathy
- delirium

Anti-amyloid medications for Alzheimer disease

What are anti-amyloid medications?
Anti-amyloid medications are disease-modifying medications that target amyloid and slow progression of Alzheimer disease by 27-35 % (months to years). They're only effective for Alzheimer disease because amyloid is unique to Alzheimer disease.

The 2 currently-approved anti-amyloid medications are:
- Leqembi (lecanemab) [IV] * approved as of 01/06/23
- Kisunla (donanemab) [IV] * approved as of 07/02/24

Why choose one anti-amyloid medication over another?
Leqembi is safer (0.4-1.4 % risk of serious brain bleed or swelling) but less effective (27 % vs 35) and more inconvenient (every 2 weeks vs 4). Kisunla is more effective (35 % vs 27) and more convenient (every 4 weeks vs 2) but less safe (2-3 times the risk of brain bleed or swelling). An 8 % difference over 4 years is 4 months.
